Prompt: "If you could time travel to observe any historical period or moment for a day, where and when would you go, and what would you hope to learn? Please explain your answer in 3-5+ full sentences."

---

Ancient Egypt — specifically somewhere around 1250 BCE, toward the later years of Ramesses II's reign — is where I'd go without much debate. The pyramids are obviously the first thing that comes to mind, but honestly, those were already ancient by then, which somehow makes it even more interesting to me. People in 1250 BCE were already looking at the Great Pyramid the same way we look at something impossibly old. That layering of history within history has always gotten under my skin.

What I actually want to see, though, isn't the monuments themselves. It's the workers. We know from limestone flakes and papyri found at Deir el-Medina that the men carving the royal tombs in the Valley of the Kings had organized work gangs, went on strike over unpaid grain rations, kept journals, and wrote letters home. They were not slaves, which surprises people every time I bring it up — they were skilled state employees with names, complaints, love lives, and bad days. I want to sit at the edge of that village and just watch for a few hours. See who argues, who's laughing, who's dragging their feet on a Monday morning.

I'd also want to get a feel for the religious life on the ground. Not the big ceremonies — the daily stuff. Did people actually feel the presence of the gods in an immediate, personal way, or was it more like background noise, something the priests handled while you went about getting bread baked and kids fed? That question genuinely keeps me up sometimes because the answer would say so much about what religion actually is versus what we write it down as being.

And the food. I know that sounds small, but I'd give a lot to taste the bread they made from emmer wheat with a cup of their beer, which by all accounts was thick, sour, and nothing like what you'd get today. The mundane stuff — what a meal looked like, who you ate with, whether there was noise or quiet — that's what disappears completely from the record. You can read about a pharaoh's military campaigns in stone. You can't read about what dinner smelled like.
