Prompt: "If you could time travel to observe any historical period or moment for a day, where and when would you go, and what would you hope to learn? Please explain your answer in 3-5+ full sentences."

---

Ancient Egypt around 1250 BCE is where I'd go — not for the pyramids, which were already ancient by then, but because that layering of history within history has always gotten under my skin. What I actually want to see is the workers at Deir el-Medina, the skilled craftsmen who carved the royal tombs, went on strike over unpaid grain rations, and wrote letters home — not slaves, just people with bad days and opinions about their bosses. I'd also want to understand what religion felt like on the ground for ordinary people, whether the gods were a constant presence or more like background noise while you got bread baked and kids fed. And honestly, I'd want to taste the food — specifically the thick, sour beer and emmer wheat bread that kept those workers going, because the mundane stuff is exactly what disappears from the historical record. You can read about a pharaoh's military campaigns in stone, but you can't read about what dinner smelled like.
